garden design brief template

Turn preferences into a checkable brief.

A garden design brief should state the primary use, fixed conditions, must-keep features, desired feeling, maintenance limit, visual references, unknowns and stopping points. It should distinguish what is known from what still needs measurement or professional advice.

Editorially reviewed August 24, 2026.

This AI-generated comparison is visual inspiration, not a measured plan, specification, safety assessment, cost estimate, local approval, plant recommendation, or construction guarantee.

Three visible decisions

Make the concept answer the real question.

01

Write the job to be done

Name the one change that would make the garden more useful.

02

List fixed and flexible items

Separate boundaries, services and retained features from movable furniture and visual preferences.

03

Define evidence and limits

Attach photos and observations, then mark dimensions, costs, plants, permissions and technical decisions as unverified until checked.

What stays unchanged

Keep the source space recognizable.

The example brief retains the door, gate, fence, mature tree, drain, path, grade, services and reference camera.

Planning checklist

Check what the image cannot know.

01

Measure routes and fixed objects

Record doors, gates, paths, furniture movement, utilities, drains, roots, and maintenance access. Do not infer clearance from the image.

02

Observe light and exposure

Note direct sun, shade, wind, overlooking, and views from inside across more than one time and season.

03

Trace water and levels

Locate falls, low points, downpipes, drains, and recurring wet areas. Preserve existing outlets.

04

Verify local choices

Check permissions, product information, plant suitability, mature size, toxicity, invasiveness, upkeep, and applicable safety requirements.

Common mistakes and stopping points

Know when the visual exercise must stop.

Stop before converting the brief into construction, purchasing or planting decisions without site measurements and appropriate verification.

Questions people ask

Garden Design Brief Template FAQ

What belongs in a garden design brief?

Include use, users, fixed conditions, must-keeps, preferences, maintenance, evidence, unknowns and stopping points.

How long should a garden brief be?

It should be concise enough to use but specific enough that two concepts can be compared against the same requirements.

Can AI write the final specification?

AI can organize a brief, but technical specifications, costs, local rules and plant choices require verification.
