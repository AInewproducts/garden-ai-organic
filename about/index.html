About Garden AI

Ideas first. Verification before action.

Garden AI is a visualization product and a library of practical garden-planning guides. We use AI images to make design directions easier to compare, while keeping a clear boundary between inspiration and facts that require measurements, local knowledge, or professional judgment.

Method last updated July 31, 2026.

Editorial method

How a guide is developed.

01

Begin with one decision

Each guide starts with a distinct reader problem, such as organizing shade, choosing the right level of patio change, or planning routes for a dog. We check that the intent is not already answered by another Garden AI page.

02

Separate design logic from site facts

We explain choices a reader can evaluate—hierarchy, routes, use, maintenance, and unchanged conditions—without inventing dimensions, prices, plant safety, climate suitability, regulations, or structural conclusions.

03

Add practical limits and stopping points

Guides identify details an image cannot establish, including drainage, utilities, boundaries, access, local rules, and product information. They also say when a reader should stop and consult an appropriate local professional.

04

Run technical and editorial checks

Before publication, pages are checked for a distinct title and heading, clear disclosures, working internal links, structured data, canonical URLs, sitemap inclusion, and desktop and mobile layout. “Editorially reviewed” means checked against this process; it does not mean expert or professional review.

AI image methodology

What the images show—and what they do not.

Our case-guide images are AI-generated visual concepts. When a guide uses a before-and-after comparison, the goal is to preserve recognizable fixed conditions and the camera viewpoint so the proposed design choices are easier to discuss. The image is reviewed for obvious continuity problems and labeled as an AI concept.

An image does not verify scale, boundaries, levels, drainage, utilities, structure, material performance, plant identity or safety, local suitability, cost, code, permits, or buildability. Apparent details may be inaccurate. Readers should use the image to clarify preferences, not as a measured plan, specification, safety assessment, or guarantee.

Corrections & scope

Claims stay bounded by available evidence.

01

No invented authority

Garden AI does not claim that its guides are written or reviewed by a landscape architect, horticulturist, engineer, veterinarian, or other licensed professional unless that is explicitly and truthfully documented.

02

No simulated first-hand experience

We do not present an AI concept as a completed Garden AI installation, field test, customer result, or personal site visit. Examples are planning aids, not evidence of construction outcomes.

03

Visible corrections

When a material error is identified, the page should be corrected and its review or modification date updated to reflect the substantive change. Dates are not changed merely to make content appear fresh.
